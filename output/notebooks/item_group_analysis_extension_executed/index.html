    Methodology: Inter-Annotator Agreement Analysis

    Background

    Inter-annotator agreement (IAA) measures the degree of consensus between multiple evaluators when assessing the same content. In the context of STREAMS checklist evaluation, IAA quantifies how consistently different reviewers assess whether microbiome research papers comply with specific reporting guidelines.

    Fixed Kappa Calculation

    Issue Resolved: The original implementation incorrectly used pairwise Cohen's Kappa calculations that only worked for single comparisons, producing unrealistic binary results (0.0 or 1.0).

    Solution Implemented: Complete rewrite to compute agreement per-paper across evaluators, then aggregate across papers. This produces realistic intermediate Kappa values representing the full spectrum of agreement quality.

    Metrics

    Cohen's Kappa (κ): A statistical measure that accounts for agreement occurring by chance. Kappa values range from -1 to +1: - κ > 0.8: Excellent agreement - 0.6 < κ ≤ 0.8: Good agreement
    - 0.4 < κ ≤ 0.6: Moderate agreement - 0.2 < κ ≤ 0.4: Fair agreement - κ ≤ 0.2: Poor agreement

    Agreement Rate: The simple percentage of cases where evaluators gave identical ratings. While easier to interpret, this metric doesn't account for chance agreement.

    Analysis Levels

    Item-Level Analysis: Examines agreement for individual checklist items (e.g., "1.1: Study design"). This identifies specific criteria where evaluators consistently agree or disagree.

    Group-Level Analysis: Aggregates agreement across related items within checklist sections (e.g., "Abstract", "Methods"). This reveals broader patterns in evaluation consistency across major reporting categories.
