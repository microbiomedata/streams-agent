# STREAMS Evaluation Instructions

This document provides comprehensive instructions for completing a STREAMS (Standards for Technical Reporting in Environmental and host-Associated Microbiome Studies) evaluation template.

## Overview

The STREAMS checklist is a standardized tool for evaluating the completeness and quality of reporting in microbiome research papers. It contains 18 main categories with 75 total items organized by typical manuscript sections.

## Template Structure

The STREAMS evaluation template is an Excel file with the following columns:

- **Number**: Item identifier (e.g., "1", "1.1", "2.3")
- **Item**: Brief description of the reporting element
- **Recommendation**: Detailed guidance on what should be reported
- **Source**: Reference source for the recommendation
- **Additional Guidance**: Extra context or clarification
- **Example(s)**: Illustrative examples of good reporting
- **Present in the manuscript? Yes/No/NA**: Your evaluation rating
- **Comments or location in manuscript**: Your detailed comments

## How to Complete an Evaluation

### Step 1: Read the Paper Thoroughly

Before beginning the evaluation, carefully read the entire manuscript including:
- Main text (all sections)
- Supplementary materials
- Data availability statements
- Acknowledgments and funding information

### Step 2: Understand the Rating System

For each checklist item, assign one of three ratings:

#### **Yes** - Item is adequately reported
- The manuscript includes the required information
- The reporting meets the STREAMS recommendation
- Information is clear and sufficient for reproducibility

#### **No** - Item is missing or inadequately reported
- The required information is completely absent
- The reporting is insufficient or unclear
- Critical details are missing that affect reproducibility

#### **NA** - Item is not applicable
- The study design doesn't require this type of reporting
- The methodology doesn't involve this aspect
- The item is irrelevant to this particular research

### Step 3: Provide Specific Comments

In the "Comments or location in manuscript" column, provide:

#### For "Yes" ratings:
- **Location**: Specify where the information appears
  - Examples: "Abstract", "Methods section 2.3", "Figure 1 caption", "Supplementary Table S2"
- **Brief validation**: Confirm what was found
  - Examples: "DNA extraction protocol described", "Sample sizes clearly stated"

#### For "No" ratings:
- **What's missing**: Specify what information is absent
  - Examples: "Sample storage conditions not described", "Quality control metrics missing"
- **Impact**: Note why this affects the evaluation
  - Examples: "Critical for reproducibility", "Needed to assess study quality"

#### For "NA" ratings:
- **Justification**: Explain why the item doesn't apply
  - Examples: "Study uses existing datasets", "No host organisms involved", "Observational study - no experimental treatments"

## Section-by-Section Guidance

### Abstract (Items 1-1.5)
Evaluate whether the abstract includes:
- Study design and objectives (1, 1.1)
- Environmental context and sample information (1.2)
- Host information if applicable (1.3)
- Methods overview (1.4)
- Key results summary (1.5)

**Common patterns:**
- Rate "Yes" if information is present, even if brief
- Rate "No" if critical details are completely absent
- Most abstracts will have mixed Yes/No ratings

### Introduction (Items 2-2.1)
Assess background and rationale:
- Clear study motivation and context (2)
- Specific hypotheses or research questions (2.1)

### Methods (Items 3-8.5)
This is the largest section covering:

#### Study Design (3-3.9)
- Overall experimental design
- Sample descriptions and selection criteria
- Environmental context and geographic information
- Temporal aspects (dates, timing)
- Host information (if applicable)
- Ethical considerations
- Sample size justification

#### Laboratory Methods (4-6.6)
- Sample storage and preservation
- DNA/RNA extraction protocols
- Library preparation details
- Quality control measures
- Controls (positive and negative)
- Sequencing platform and parameters

#### Bioinformatics (7-7.9)
- Data processing pipelines
- Quality control steps
- Database versions and parameters
- Statistical methods
- Handling of missing data
- Bias and confounding considerations

#### Data Availability (8-8.5)
- Metadata accessibility
- Raw data deposition
- Processed data availability
- Code and software accessibility
- Reproducibility measures

### Results (Items 9-10.3)
Evaluate reporting of:
- Sample characteristics and experimental outcomes
- Sequencing data quality and statistics
- Statistical analysis results
- Figure and table quality

### Discussion (Items 11-11.3)
Assess:
- Summary of key findings
- Interpretation in context
- Study limitations
- Generalizability

### Other Elements (Items 12-18)
- Future research directions
- Conclusions
- Acknowledgments and funding
- Conflicts of interest
- Supplementary materials
- Data availability statements
- AI usage disclosure

## Best Practices for Evaluators

### Be Consistent
- Apply the same standards across all items
- Use similar comment styles throughout
- Maintain consistent interpretation of "adequate" reporting

### Be Specific
- Provide exact locations (section numbers, figure captions, etc.)
- Quote key phrases when helpful
- Reference specific tables or supplementary files

### Be Fair
- Consider the study type and scope
- Recognize that some items may not apply to all studies
- Focus on whether information is present and sufficient, not perfect

### Common Comment Patterns
Based on analysis of evaluator exemplars:

**For location references:**
- "Abstract" (most common)
- "Materials and Methods" / "Methods"
- "Results"
- "Discussion"
- "Figure 1 caption"
- "Supplementary Table S1"
- "Data Availability"

**For missing items:**
- "Not included"
- "Not mentioned"
- "Insufficient detail"
- "Not clearly stated"

**For partial compliance:**
- "Partially described"
- "Some information provided but incomplete"
- "Brief mention but lacks detail"

## Quality Control

### Self-Check Questions
Before submitting your evaluation:

1. **Completeness**: Did I rate every applicable item?
2. **Consistency**: Are my ratings consistent with my comments?
3. **Specificity**: Did I provide specific locations for "Yes" ratings?
4. **Justification**: Did I explain "No" and "NA" ratings?
5. **Fairness**: Did I consider the study context appropriately?

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- **Over-penalizing**: Don't rate "No" for minor omissions if core information is present
- **Under-evaluating**: Don't rate "Yes" for vague or insufficient reporting
- **Inconsistent NA usage**: Apply NA consistently for truly non-applicable items
- **Vague comments**: Avoid comments like "unclear" without specifics
- **Missing locations**: Always specify where you found information for "Yes" ratings

## Template Completion Workflow

1. **Setup**: Open the manuscript and STREAMS template side-by-side
2. **Initial scan**: Quickly review the paper structure and identify major sections
3. **Systematic evaluation**: Go through items 1-18 sequentially
4. **Cross-reference**: Check supplementary materials for missing information
5. **Review**: Double-check ratings and comments for consistency
6. **Final validation**: Ensure all applicable items are completed

## Inter-Evaluator Agreement

When multiple evaluators assess the same paper:
- Focus on objective presence/absence of information
- Discuss borderline cases with other evaluators
- Document reasoning for difficult decisions
- Aim for consistency in interpretation standards

This systematic approach ensures comprehensive, fair, and reproducible STREAMS evaluations that advance the quality of microbiome research reporting.
